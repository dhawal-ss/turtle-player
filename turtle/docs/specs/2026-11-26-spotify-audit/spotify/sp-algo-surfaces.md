## Mechanics notes

CADENCE: DW 30 tracks Mondays; RR 30 songs Fridays (2018-11). Daily Mix cadence drifted across Spotify's own pages: 2018-05 "update daily"; 2018-11 refreshes "within a day of when you last listened to it"; the CURRENT support page says only "The more you listen, the more frequently they update" — the daily guarantee is gone. RR GATING (undated S4A page; the only documented never-heard rule here): out if the listener already played it; eligible "up to 4 weeks if a listener hasn't heard it", a bounded per-listener window rather than one Friday; one song per artist per listener per week; main/featured only, "not remixer"; no Various Artists; no re-releases; acoustic/karaoke/live out, remixes in; deliver >=7 days pre-release for week one; a pitch >=7 days out picks WHICH song, else Spotify picks. DW FRESHNESS: no documented never-heard filter. 2025-01 calls it blending "songs you might have missed over the years with recent releases"; the 2015 post kept a bug letting "semi-familiar content" through to build trust. Documented floor: one play with no replay or heart does not count. DAILY MIX: "clustering technology to identify distinct subgroupings within our users' listening patterns"; up to six mixes, count scaling with listening variety; NOT predefined genres, it "reflects how the user actually listens"; "only a small amount of discovery"; Premium loads open-ended. TASTE PROFILE: Spotify-side, not viewable. Inputs: recent listens, top tracks/artists, user embeddings, interactions. Filtering sits inside the taste-profile systems; Home Shortcuts and Recently Played read unfiltered. Exclusion is playlist/track scoped, past+future, live in 48h, DW next week; the track played elsewhere still counts. ARCHITECTURE: centralized exploration spends a fixed impression budget on new content, then hands exploitation recommenders raw signals, new-content vectors and personalized candidate pools for two-stage retrieval+ranking (~10x listeners in A/B). DW optimises discovery; Daily Mix is favourites-led; a DW-formed user-creator link can propagate into that user's Daily Mixes. Embeddings: audio encoder + collaborative encoder (playlist co-occurrence). Sequencing research optimises ORDER: position-aware + local-sequential, +2.52% completions, -2.73% skips. ALGOTORIAL: "our editors pick the pool of songs for the algorithms to select from for each listener". Personalized playlists carry a "Made for [username]" byline; DJ, Smart Shuffle and Blends do not.

## Spotify weaknesses

- **The taste profile is a black box the listener cannot read** — Spotify's own engineering post says the taste profile is "not something you can view or retrieve". A listener cannot see why a track was recommended, what the system believes about them, or which signals dominate. Every correction is therefore blind: you act, wait, and guess whether it landed. (https://engineering.atspotify.com/2023/10/exclude-from-your-taste-profile)
- **Hide and Snooze do not reach the model that makes the recommendations** — Spotify documents that neither Hide nor Snooze affects the taste profile. Rejecting a track only suppresses it on the surface where you rejected it, so the strongest, cheapest negative signal a listener can give is deliberately discarded from the shared model driving Discover Weekly and Daily Mix. (https://support.spotify.com/article/exclude-playlists-or-tracks-from-your-taste-profile/)
- **Corrections take up to 48 hours, and a week to reach Discover Weekly** — Excluding a playlist or track "will take effect within 48 hours" and "Some recommended content such as Discover Weekly may not be affected until the following week". There is no immediate feedback loop: a listener who fixes a mistaken signal on Monday may not see the result until the next Monday. (https://support.spotify.com/article/exclude-playlists-or-tracks-from-your-taste-profile/)
- **Snooze is Premium-only, so free listeners cannot mute a track at all** — The 2025-09-05 controls post lists Snooze — pausing a track from recommendations for 30 days — as Premium only. Free listeners get Hide, which is scoped to a single playlist and does not touch the taste profile, leaving them no way to suppress a track across recommendations. (https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-09-05/new-user-controls-personalize-listening/)
- **Discover Weekly cannot be freely re-rolled: only Spotify's five presets** — The 2025 controls let a listener pick from "up to five genre options, personalized based on your listening history". Spotify chooses the five. There is no free-form prompt, no plain regenerate, no way to exclude a genre, and no way to ask for a slice outside those presets. (https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-06-30/discover-weekly-turns-10-celebrating-100-billion-tracks-streamed-and-a-decade-of-personalized-discovery/)
- **No documented freshness guarantee for Discover Weekly** — Spotify documents an already-heard exclusion only for Release Radar. For Discover Weekly its own engineering post records that "semi-familiar content" was deliberately kept, and the 2025 tips post describes it as blending older songs you missed with new releases. A listener wanting strictly unheard music has no setting for it. (https://engineering.atspotify.com/2015/11/what-made-discover-weekly-one-of-our-most-successful-feature-launches-to-date)
- **Daily Mix is designed to be low-discovery and has no published contract** — Spotify says Daily Mix "emphasizes familiar and even favorite music with only a small amount of discovery". The current support page states no track count, no refresh day and no feedback controls — only "The more you listen, the more frequently they update" — so a listener cannot rely on it refreshing at all. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/spotify-mixes/)
- **A paid commission signal is injected into Daily Mix, Radio and Autoplay** — Discovery Mode lets rights holders pay a commission on streams in exchange for Spotify adding "a signal to the algorithms that shape personalized listening sessions". Listed contexts include Daily, Artist, Mood, Decade and Genre Mixes plus Radio and Autoplay. Listeners cannot see which tracks carry the signal or turn it off, and the rate is unpublished. (https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/discovery-mode-contexts/)
- **Release Radar structurally hides reissues, live and acoustic versions** — Spotify excludes re-releases of songs already on Spotify and "some alternative versions of tracks, like acoustic, karaoke, or live versions", and caps each listener at one song per artist per week. A listener following a catalogue-heavy or live-oriented artist will never see those releases, regardless of taste. (https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/getting-music-on-release-radar/)
- **Editor-seeded pools are undisclosed, so the algorithm's real reach is unknown** — Spotify states that "For some personalized playlists, our editors pick the pool of songs for the algorithms to select from for each listener" but never says WHICH playlists, how large the pools are, or how selection works inside them. A listener cannot tell whether a "Made for you" playlist is open-catalogue or a human shortlist. (https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/types-of-spotify-playlists)
- **Opting out of a signal requires foresight, not hindsight** — Private Session must be switched on before listening to keep plays out of Discover Weekly, and taste-profile exclusion is scoped to playlists and tracks — the same track played from Liked Songs still counts. There is no documented way to retroactively remove an arbitrary listening session from the model. (https://newsroom.spotify.com/2019-05-02/five-ways-to-make-your-discover-weekly-playlists-even-more-personalized/)
- **Reported: repetition, resurfaced hidden tracks and generic filler** — Spotify Community threads report Discover Weekly reusing the same tracks across weeks, resurfacing songs the user had hidden, delivering unplayed genres, and serving short generic instrumental tracks users attribute to AI generation. Anecdotal user reports, not measured; the only Spotify reply found was that it often tests variations. (https://community.spotify.com/t5/Your-Library/Discover-Weekly-has-become-unusable/m-p/7110287/highlight/true)

## Unverifiable

- The three-model account of Discover Weekly (collaborative filtering + NLP over web text + raw-audio CNN). No Spotify page states that trio for DW; it documents latent-factor vectors, user embeddings and an audio+collaborative encoder pair separately.
- Daily Mix track count. The commonly cited "15-30 tracks" comes from press and community posts. Spotify documents only that Premium "will load more songs as needed" and gives no number for free users or on the current support page.
- The "about two weeks of listening" threshold before Daily Mix appears. Spotify says only that it is "available to all users who we know enough about to generate one", with no stated minimum.
- Whether Discover Weekly and Release Radar are actually excluded from Discovery Mode. The contexts page simply does not list them; it names no exclusions, so their status is unstated rather than confirmed either way.
- Whether Discover Weekly applies any already-heard filter at all. No Spotify page states one, and the 2015 engineering post indicates semi-familiar tracks were deliberately permitted.
- The "editors seed 30-50 core tracks" figure for algotorial playlists. Third-party marketing blogs only; Spotify confirms editor-chosen pools exist but publishes no size.
- Loud & Clear editorial-vs-algorithmic stream splits (31%/9%, ~18% of playlist-driven streams, 70% of top-50 editorial adds to major-affiliated artists). None appear on the Loud & Clear site, which states only that it analysed 2025 royalty data.
- The claim that 72% of Discover Weekly recommendations already appear in the listener's history. Traced to one SEO page citing "MusicTech Analytics" with no primary source.
- Whether the 2023 optimisation-based sequencing method and the 2024 centralized exploration system are deployed in production specifically for Discover Weekly, Release Radar or Daily Mix. Both are Spotify-owned research; neither says so.
- Daily Mix's launch date (widely given as 2016) and whether the Discover Weekly genre controls reached free-tier listeners. The 2025-09-15 free-experience post names Discover Weekly and Release Radar but states no tier availability for the controls.
