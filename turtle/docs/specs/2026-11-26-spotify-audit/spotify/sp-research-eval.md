## Mechanics notes

SHIP RULE (2024-03-05): ship iff significantly superior on >=1 success metric; significantly non-inferior on ALL guardrails; no evidence of deterioration on any success/guardrail/deterioration metric; no quality test invalidates the experiment. Quality tests include sample ratio mismatch and pre-exposure bias. ERROR BUDGET sized to the conjunction. S success, G guardrail, Q quality, T extra deterioration tests. Deterioration+quality: a_minus* = a_minus/(S+G+Q+T). Success: a* = a/S. Guardrail alpha uncorrected. Power: beta* = (beta - a_minus)/((1 - a_minus)(G+1)), simplified beta/(G+1). Guardrails must ALL pass at once, so 5 independent guardrails at 80% power give joint power under 40%, 10 give about 11%. Inferiority-only guardrails need no extra sample; non-inferiority needs a stated margin (example: 1% NIM on sales amount). SEQUENTIAL: group sequential testing, approximating mSPRT. Frequentist only; empirical-Bayes shrinkage with automatic FDR control rejected as needing a per-metric prior over sometimes >200 experiments. EXPOSURE AND PIPELINE: typed properties (enum/int) in YAML beside code, assigned by PlanOut policies; only non-default values sent, clients keyed by version. Exposure = Config Assigned AND Config Applied, optionally narrowed by a custom exposure source such as a page visit. Values refresh in background but apply only on app relaunch, so the experience never changes mid-session and feedback lags a relaunch. Metrics Catalog: SQL pipelines join metric data to group membership, aggregate into an OLAP cube, served at sub-second latency. LONG-TERM PROXIES: survival model treats engagement as time-to-event with a churn-based time-to-inactivity metric. LOPE decomposes the reward and uses short-term rewards for variance reduction under a weaker-than-surrogacy assumption. ForTune runs offline what-if scenarios over user data plus business-metric estimates, hinging on feature selection. HOME BANDIT (launched Mar 2025): action = target distribution over content types; reward = Home engagement; epsilon-greedy; context = time of day, day of week, device type, user and content embeddings. Slate built greedily item by item, each step trading relevance score against a KL penalty to the predicted distribution. Metrics: i2s = streams/impressions; activity = share of users starting a stream from Home; consumption = total minutes streamed.

## Spotify weaknesses

- **The deployed Home policy is rewarded on short-term engagement; long-term satisfaction is future work** — The contextual-bandit calibration policy launched on Home in March 2025 uses Home engagement as its reward and is judged on i2s, activity and consumption. Multi-objective rewards that include long-term satisfaction are listed as future work, not as shipped. So the live ranking objective on Spotify's most important surface is explicitly a short-horizon one. (https://research.atspotify.com/2025/9/calibrated-recommendations-with-contextual-bandits-on-spotify-homepage)
- **Personalization measurably narrows an individual listener's range** — Spotify's own randomized field experiment found that switching from demographic-popularity podcast recommendations to history-based personalization raised podcast streams 28.90% while cutting individual-level diversity 11.51% (aggregate diversity rose 5.96%). The authors call this an engagement-diversity trade-off and a Balkanization pattern, with spillover into untouched parts of the app. (https://research.atspotify.com/publications/the-engagement-diversity-connection-evidence-from-a-field-experiment-on-spotify)
- **Algorithmic listening is less diverse than organic, and users diversify by leaving it** — Spotify reports that algorithmically driven listening is associated with reduced consumption diversity, while high diversity is strongly associated with conversion and retention. Users who become more diverse over time do so by moving away from algorithmic consumption and increasing organic consumption. The recommender is on the wrong side of the metric Spotify says predicts long-term value. (https://research.atspotify.com/publications/algorithmic-effects-on-the-diversity-of-consumption-on-spotify)
- **The outcome that matters is not observable inside an experiment** — Retention is not observable by the end of an A/B test, so every long-horizon decision runs on a proxy: survival models with a churn-based time-to-inactivity metric (2022), LOPE off-policy estimates (2024), and ForTune offline scenarios (2025), which the paper itself says cannot fully replace online experiments. Three successive proxy layers, none with a published accuracy figure. (https://research.atspotify.com/publications/ForTune-Running-Offline-Scenarios-to-Estimate-Impact-on-Business-Metrics)
- **Only about 12% of experiments win, and 42% are rolled back** — Spotify's published figures: win rate about 12%, learning rate about 64%, and guardrails trigger rollbacks in 42% of experiments. Even with a mature platform and 250+ Home tests a year, roughly seven in eight changes do not ship as wins, and nearly half of those tried are reversed because something else regressed. (https://confidence.spotify.com/blog/experiments-with-learning)
- **No automatic false-discovery control, by deliberate choice** — Spotify names a calibrated empirical-Bayes prior as the one Bayesian configuration offering a real advantage, because it gives shrinkage and automatic false discovery rate control, then rejects it as too costly: a prior per metric, backfilled over sometimes more than 200 experiments. Multiplicity is handled by manual alpha-splitting rather than by shrinkage. (https://engineering.atspotify.com/2026/9/why-spotify-is-not-using-bayesian-a-b-testing)
- **Guardrail coverage is bought with statistical power** — Because every guardrail must pass simultaneously, five independent guardrails at 80% power each give a joint pass rate below 40%, and ten give about 11%. Spotify's own correction is beta/(G+1). Adding safety metrics therefore directly inflates the sample and duration needed, which caps how many things an experiment can be asked to protect. (https://engineering.atspotify.com/2024/03/risk-aware-product-decisions-in-a-b-tests-with-multiple-metrics)
- **The LLM judges Spotify now uses for offline evaluation are biased by the stated objective** — Spotify's own 2026 result: telling an LLM judge what an algorithm optimizes for changes its scores on identical recommendations, raising diversity scores by up to 0.89 points (Cohen's d = 1.82, p < 1e-18) across four frontier models from three providers, with bias size varying by model. The proposed remedy is hiding algorithm metadata, not fixing the judge. (https://research.atspotify.com/publications/intent-description-anchoring-bias-in-llm-as-a-judge-evaluation-of-recommendation-systems)
- **LLM-judge agreement is only moderate per item, and never validated against online outcomes** — Spotify reports item-level agreement with human labels as moderate overall; the strong result is at system-ranking level (Kendall's tau up to 0.92 for nDCG@100 across 52 configurations). Neither the Cranfield study nor the profile-aware podcast judge (47 participants) reports a comparison against A/B or interaction outcomes. (https://research.atspotify.com/publications/from-ir-to-recsys-evaluating-llm-based-judges-in-cranfield-style-recommendation-collections)
- **The benchmarks the field uses do not detect the modelling capability they justify** — Spotify authors showed two trivial recency-weighted pairwise probes beat Transformer reproductions by 15-38% on three Amazon datasets, 4.4% on MovieLens-1M and 9-28% on four more, losing only on MovieLens-20M (-27.3%). They conclude these widely used sequential-recommendation benchmarks are poorly suited to measuring gains from higher-order sequence modelling. (https://research.atspotify.com/publications/do-sequential-recommendation-benchmarks-really-require-higher-order-sequence-modelling)
- **Headline experimentation statistics are published without any methodology** — The 42% rollback rate, the ~12% win rate and the ~64% learning rate all appear on Spotify-owned product pages with no definition of a rollback, no formula for either rate, no time period and no sample. They are also marketing for Confidence, and I found no independent audit of them. (https://confidence.spotify.com/blog/better-decisions-with-guardrails)
- **Skipping is measured per track, not per listener, and has no published formal definition** — Spotify's skip research models the distribution of stop times for a track (its skip profile), aggregated over billions of streams, with no formal skip definition and no unique-user count reported. It answers which second of a song loses listeners, not whether a given listener was satisfied, and no Spotify page I fetched states a platform-wide skip rate. (https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0239418)

## Unverifiable

- WebSearch budget ran out mid-research (per-turn limit shared across agents). Not searched: Spotify survey instruments, interleaving status after 2023, 2026 Confidence changelogs, any published discovery rate. The rest used direct fetches only.
- No Spotify-owned page I fetched states a platform-wide skip rate or discovery rate. The 14.1% per-platform and 52% skip figures seen in search results are non-Spotify student and blog analyses and are deliberately excluded.
- loudandclear.byspotify.com (2025 royalty data) carries no discovery statistics: no share of listening that is discovery, no artists-discovered count, no editorial-versus-algorithmic split. Only a '10 years of Fresh Finds' framing.
- Spotify's actual alpha, beta, minimum detectable effect and sequential stopping thresholds are not published. The 2026 Bayesian post names GST and mSPRT and shows simulated false-positive curves but gives no figures in text.
- Whether interleaving shipped on Home. It was described as planned in June 2023 and I found no later Spotify page confirming deployment, so it must not be treated as a live capability.
- Agreement statistics for the profile-aware podcast LLM judge are not on the publication page: only 47 participants, 90 days of history, and a claim of matching human judgments with high fidelity.
- Quantitative accuracy of ForTune, LOPE and the RIPS-style slate estimator. The publication pages report no error metrics, effect sizes, or margins over baselines. Full PDFs were not fetched.
- Market and subscription-tier scope of the March 2025 Home calibration-bandit launch is not stated, so market_limited was left unset rather than guessed for that entry.
- A search snippet claimed Spotify includes critical business metrics as deterioration metrics in all experiments. The fetched 2024 risk-aware post does not confirm 'all experiments', so the claim is excluded.
- Definitions of 'podcast accuracy' and 'overall accuracy' in the Home bandit offline evaluation are not given, so those offline percentages cannot be reproduced or compared across work.
