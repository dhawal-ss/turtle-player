## Mechanics notes

NORMALIZATION: loudness is measured once at upload, the stored file is never altered, and gain is applied at playback. Target -14 LUFS (ITU 1770). Loud masters get negative gain; soft masters get positive gain capped by their own true peak - Spotify's example raises a -20 LUFS master with -5 dBFS TP only to -16 LUFS, i.e. gain = min(target - measured, -1 - truePeak), with 1 dB reserved as headroom for lossy encodings. Album coherence: one gain for a whole album so compensation never shifts between its tracks; shuffling an album, or mixing albums as in a playlist, falls back to per-track gain. An equivalent needs integrated LUFS + true peak per track, one pair per album, and a context flag choosing between them. Listener levels: Loud -11, Normal -14, Quiet -19 LUFS (Premium). Only Loud inserts a limiter: -1 dB threshold on sample values, 5 ms attack, 100 ms decay, applied regardless of true peak, to stop clipping on soft material. Normalization also runs on lossless FLAC and the levels apply to all formats, so there is no bit-exact path while it is on. Web player and third-party devices (TVs, speakers) skip normalization entirely. Listener controls are a 'Normalize volume' toggle plus a 'Volume level' picker under Settings > Playback. QUALITY: Low ~24, Normal ~96, High ~160, Very high ~320 kbit/s, Lossless up to 24-bit/44.1 kHz FLAC - all worded 'equivalent to approximately', no codec named for desktop or mobile. Automatic/auto-adjust is the default and lowers quality to avoid stuttering. Settings are per network (Wi-Fi vs cellular), per purpose (stream vs download) and per device, including Connect targets, which are changed from the phone (device icon > Change quality settings). Lossless needs mobile 9.0.58+ / desktop 1.2.67+ and a steady 1.5-2 Mbps, excludes music videos, podcasts and audiobooks, and prompts 'Update to Lossless' on files already downloaded. The Platinum plan page gives the only published rate figure: up to 840 kbps. TRANSITIONS: crossfade overlaps two tracks on a bare slider with no published range; gapless is a separate 'Track transitions' toggle; Automix beat-matches on select Spotify playlists only and survives Shuffle. None of the three can be set while playing over Spotify Connect. Mix (beta 2025-08-19) adds per-pair authored transitions: Auto, or Fade/Rise presets with volume, EQ and effects, over a waveform with beat data, showing each track's BPM and Camelot key so the listener can reorder for compatible transitions.

## Spotify weaknesses

- **Lossless stops at 44.1 kHz: no hi-res sample rates** — Spotify's ceiling is 24-bit/44.1 kHz FLAC, which is CD sample rate at greater bit depth. Apple Music, TIDAL and Qobuz publish up to 24-bit/192 kHz. Spotify's own newsroom post and Connect page both state 44.1 kHz as the maximum, so this is a committed product limit, not a rollout stage. (https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-09-10/lossless-listening-arrives-on-spotify-premium-with-a-richer-more-detailed-listening-experience/)
- **No lossless over Bluetooth, by Spotify's own admission** — Spotify states that Bluetooth does not provide enough bandwidth to transmit lossless audio, so the signal has to be compressed before being sent, and recommends wired or Connect instead. Spotify ships no LDAC, LHDC or aptX Lossless path of its own, so most listeners on wireless earbuds get a re-encoded stream. (https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-09-10/lossless-listening-arrives-on-spotify-premium-with-a-richer-more-detailed-listening-experience/)
- **Lossless is off by default and must be set on every device** — Nothing opts a listener in. Lossless has to be chosen separately for Wi-Fi, for cellular and for downloads, on each device, and again per Connect target from the phone. Files already downloaded stay at the old quality until the listener accepts an 'Update to Lossless' prompt, so a library silently remains lossy. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/lossless-audio-quality/)
- **Lossless excluded from music videos, podcasts and audiobooks** — Spotify states plainly that lossless is unavailable on music videos, podcasts or audiobooks. Podcasts sit at roughly 96 kbit/s everywhere except the web player, and drop to about 24 kbit/s on mobile when music quality is set to Low, so spoken-word quality is coupled to an unrelated music setting. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/lossless-audio-quality/)
- **Web player is a second-class client: AAC 256 max, no controls, no normalization** — The browser client is capped at AAC 128 free / AAC 256 Premium, cannot have its quality changed at all, has no lossless path, and is excluded from loudness normalization. A listener on a work machine or an unsupported OS has no way to reach what they pay for. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/audio-quality/)
- **Loudness normalization silently absent on TVs and speakers** — Spotify states the web player and third-party devices such as TVs and speakers do not use loudness normalization. Volume therefore jumps between tracks on exactly the living-room and multi-room hardware Spotify markets Connect for, with no indication in the UI that the setting has stopped applying. (https://support.spotify.com/artists/article/loudness-normalization)
- **Crossfade, gapless and Automix all stop working over Spotify Connect** — Spotify's transitions page states flatly that you cannot set these features when using Spotify Connect. The transition behaviour a listener configured on their phone disappears the moment playback moves to a speaker, and the page offers no explanation or workaround. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/tracks-transitions/)
- **Crossfade is a single global slider with no published range** — Spotify documents no seconds range, no per-playlist or per-genre override, no behaviour difference between a live album and a shuffled mix, and no way to exempt continuous records. The only documented control is 'drag the slider'. Community reports of a 1-12 s range and of a desktop bug that resets the value are unofficial. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/tracks-transitions/)
- **Gapless playback has no documented desktop path** — The transitions page gives a mobile and tablet path for the gapless toggle and a desktop path for crossfade and Automix, but none for gapless. Whether desktop listeners can control gapless at all is undocumented, which for classical, live and DJ-mix albums is the single most audible defect. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/tracks-transitions/)
- **Automix is editorially gated to 'select Spotify playlists'** — Beat-matched transitions do not work on a listener's own playlists, on albums, on artist radio or on the catalogue at large. They apply only to playlists Spotify chose. The listener's own collection, the thing they curated, is the one place the feature does not run. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/tracks-transitions/)
- **Lossless re-gated behind a pricier plan in restructured markets** — Where Premium was split into Lite, Standard and Platinum, Standard lists only 'Very high audio quality (up to 320kbps)' and lossless appears solely under Platinum, alongside Mix and the DJ integrations. 'Lossless is included in Premium at no extra cost' is therefore untrue in those markets, and Platinum members may change plan only once every 12 months. (https://support.spotify.com/za-en/article/premium-standard/)
- **No EQ Spotify can vouch for, and no speed or pitch control on music** — Spotify's support articles document no equalizer at all; on Android the control exists only if the handset maker exposed the system panel. Playback speed is documented for podcasts and audiobooks only, with no values listed, and community answers state music and local files have no speed control. Nothing is documented for skip-silence or a sleep timer on music. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/podcasts-and-shows/)

## Unverifiable

- Which codec each desktop/mobile tier actually uses. No Spotify page maps Ogg Vorbis or AAC to the 24/96/160/320 kbit/s levels; the support pages name a codec only for the web player (AAC).
- Forum claim that each master is transcoded six times (3x Ogg Vorbis, 2x AAC, 1x HE-AACv2) and that HE-AACv2 serves the ~24 kbit/s Low tier. No Spotify engineering or support page found stating this.
- The crossfade slider range. Community posts say 1-12 s, with 0 s disabling it on mobile but not desktop; Spotify's own transitions page publishes no range at all.
- Whether the desktop 'hardware acceleration' toggle affects audio decoding or only interface rendering, and whether it still exists in Settings. Community threads contradict each other and report it moved to the Help menu.
- The iOS equalizer's band count and preset list, and which Android manufacturers expose the Equalizer entry. No current Spotify support article documents the EQ.
- Podcast playback speed step values. Community posts list 0.5x, 0.8x, 1x, 1.5x, 2x, 3x (elsewhere 3.5x); Spotify's own page documents only the 1x control.
- The full list of the 50+ markets where lossless is available. Spotify named 12 at launch and later said 'more than 50 markets' without enumerating them.
- Premium Platinum's prices and its exact market list (reported as India, Indonesia, UAE, Saudi Arabia, South Africa from Nov 2025). The support pages describe the plan but state neither price nor availability.
- Whether loudness normalization is on by default. The normalization page never says, and the accessibility page documents the toggle without stating its default state.
- Whether Mix transitions survive playback over Spotify Connect or Bluetooth. Spotify's posts do not say, and third-party guides contradict each other.
