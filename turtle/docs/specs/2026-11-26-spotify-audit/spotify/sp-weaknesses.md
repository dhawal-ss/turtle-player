## Mechanics notes

WebSearch budget for this run was exhausted before my first query, so everything here comes from direct fetches of public pages. Press-only items are in "unverifiable". EXPORT PATH. Two self-serve packages plus one on request, delivered as a ZIP with a "Read Me First" file. Account Data: playlists, library, search queries with clicked URIs, ad "Inferences", Taste Profiles, 19 Wrapped metrics — but streaming history only for the past year. Extended Streaming History: the account's lifetime, one record per play, 21 documented items (end time UTC, username, platform, ms played, country, IP, user agent, track/artist/album, track URI, episode/show name, episode URI, reason started, reason ended, shuffle, skipped, offline, offline timestamp, private session). The public page prints no JSON keys, so snake_case names (ts, ms_played, reason_start, reason_end, skipped, offline, incognito_mode, spotify_track_uri) are inferred. For an importer: ms played gives true listen time, reason-started/reason-ended plus skipped give skip intent, offline and private session mark the plays Spotify itself discounts from taste. WRAPPED COUNTING. Window 1 Jan to mid-November. Top songs: a listen counts once past 30 seconds, each stream counted once regardless of track length. Top artists: weighted stream count, primary artist above secondary/featured, so an artist can rank with no top song. Top albums: counts when most tracks were streamed from anywhere, scored on total streams and evenness of spread. Non-music (white noise, nature) filtered; private-mode and taste-excluded plays dropped from taste stories; offline plays included. Entry gate: 30 songs over 30 seconds each, 5 distinct artists. HOME CONTROLS (12 Nov 2026, mobile only, Free and Premium, global). Press-and-hold then "Remove from this section" on a Home tile or a Recents item is explicitly cosmetic — no effect on future recommendations. Only expanded "Not interested", now covering shows and authors, feeds back. Taste Profile (explicit more/less) is US + NZ Premium beta. MIGRATION. TuneMyMusic and Soundiiz read playlists, liked songs and albums; artists do not transfer to YouTube Music. Matching is title + artist + album + ISRC; misses are labelled "Missing" with a CSV. YouTube Music caps a playlist at 5,000 tracks and splits longer ones. Soundiiz free: one playlist per transfer, 200 tracks each, one sync a day, favorites paid-only ($39/yr). Nobody moves play history — only the Extended Streaming History ZIP has it.

## Spotify weaknesses

- **Two of the three new Home controls are cosmetic by design** — Spotify states that "Remove from this section" on a Home tile and on Recents only cleans up the feed and does not affect future recommendations. Only expanded "Not interested" shapes recommendations, and all three are mobile-only. (https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-11-12/home-feed-updates/)
- **The real taste control is a two-market Premium beta** — Taste Profile, the only documented way to say what you want more or less of, is in beta for eligible Premium listeners in the U.S. and New Zealand only. Everyone else gets per-item hiding and "Not interested". (https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-11-12/home-feed-updates/)
- **Spotify's own research treats habitual, repetitive listening as the baseline** — The GLIDE paper's headline result is raising non-habitual podcast streaming on Home by up to 5.4% and new-show discovery by up to 14.3%. A gain that size implies how narrow the default was, and the page reports no diversity metrics at all. (https://research.atspotify.com/publications/from-habits-to-discovery-deploying-llms-personalized-generative-recommendations-spotify)
- **Discovery Mode is pay-for-placement inside personalized recommendations with no disclosed rate** — Spotify's own page says only that "The commission is a percentage of revenue generated on those select streams" and names no figure. No listener-facing label or disclosure for a Discovery Mode recommendation is documented anywhere on the page. (https://artists.spotify.com/en/discovery-mode)
- **No public field-level spec for the export, and no stated delivery time** — The Understanding My Data page prints 21 plain-language items for Extended Streaming History but no JSON key names; those live in a Read Me file inside the ZIP. No page states how long any of the three packages takes to arrive. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/understanding-my-data/)
- **The easy export gives one year of history; lifetime history is a separate ask** — Account Data's Streaming History covers only the past year. Anyone wanting their full listening past has to know that Extended Streaming History exists and request it as a distinct package. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/understanding-my-data/)
- **The documented export has no audiobook listening fields** — Extended Streaming History's 21 items cover tracks, podcast episodes and shows. No audiobook fields are listed, although Spotify sells audiobooks and puts a Top Audiobook Genre in Wrapped, so audiobook listening appears unexportable or the page lags the ZIP. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/understanding-my-data/)
- **Offline listening is leased, not held** — Downloads can be removed if a device goes 30 days without connecting, a sixth device strips downloads from the least recently used one, reinstalling clears everything, and individual songs cannot be downloaded at all — only playlists, albums and podcasts. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/listen-offline/)
- **Local files are second-class on mobile** — There is no sync: files are copied by hand into a Spotify folder over Files, iCloud, USB or iTunes. No supported formats are documented, crossfade works only when two files share sample rate and channel count, and the iOS folder vanishes if its Help file is deleted. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/local-files/)
- **Lossless is capped, partial and connection-fragile** — Up to 24-bit/44.1 kHz only, excluded from music videos, podcasts and audiobooks, requires Spotify Connect on third-party devices with Bluetooth and AUX warned as possibly unsupported, needs 1.5–2 Mbps, multi-room still rolling out, and no market list is published. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/lossless-audio-quality/)
- **Wrapped drops the last six weeks of the year, and Spotify had to explain itself two days after launch** — The window runs 1 January to mid-November. Spotify published "Surprised by Your 2025 Wrapped?" on 5 December, two days after the 3 December launch, attributing surprise to its own ranking choices, forgotten listening, large catalogs and shared devices. (https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-12-05/wrapped-methodology-explained/)
- **No published accessibility conformance claim** — Spotify's accessibility page states an aspiration, a plan and a feedback link, but no WCAG version or level, no VPAT or Accessibility Conformance Report, no list of known limitations and no last-updated date. (https://www.spotify.com/us/accessibility/)

## Unverifiable

- Car Thing: the end-of-support date, whether the device was bricked, and refunds. Three support URL attempts returned 404 and WebSearch was unavailable, so no primary page was reached.
- Discovery Mode's actual commission percentage. Spotify's own page states only "a percentage of revenue"; the widely reported reduced-royalty figure could not be sourced here.
- Premium price increases in 2025 and 2026 by market. The Q4 2025 earnings post says nothing about pricing, and the newsroom announcements listing surfaced no price-change headline.
- Lossless launch date and the HiFi-announcement-to-launch delay history. The 2025 newsroom post is not reachable from the listing pages I could load, so the 'launched' field is left empty.
- Wrapped 2024 backlash specifics, including the AI-generated podcast recap and its reception. Press-only, and not sourceable without web search.
- Ghost artists / Perfect Fit Content and the AI-slop controversy, including the Harper's reporting. Press-only, not sourceable here.
- Spotify's 2025 AI music policy, impersonation rules, music spam filter and any takedown counts. The safety-at-spotify page lists only 2022-2024 items; the Jan 2026 payouts post names the problem but not the policy.
- Dark patterns, cancellation-flow complaints, the iOS in-app-purchase restrictions and any regulatory action. No primary page reached.
- Library and playlist size limits. Two Spotify pages state none; any specific cap would be recall, not evidence. Search quality for obscure or long-tail catalogues is likewise unsourced.
- Third-party accessibility audits or complaints about Spotify's apps, and whether an ACR/VPAT exists elsewhere. Also unsourced: how long each export package actually takes to arrive.
