## Mechanics notes

DOWNLOAD UNIT is a container, never a track: album, playlist or show, so one song is downloadable only after being put in a playlist. ENTITLEMENT is a per-device lease: 10,000 tracks per device, 5 devices. A 6th device does not error - Spotify revokes the stalest lease: "Downloads are removed from the device you've used least recently." Spotify contradicts itself: the benefits page says 10,000 "across up to 5 devices", so the account ceiling reads as 10k or 50k. LEASE RENEWAL: "You need to go online at least once every 30 days to keep your downloads" - the check-in confirms an active subscription and reports plays for royalties. On expiry local media is deleted; playlist membership survives server-side, so the user re-downloads. No countdown UI is documented (community reports one). TWO STORAGE POOLS. (a) Cache: partial music/podcast data for smooth playback; soft-capped by a "Spotify recommended limit, which is automatically managed", overridable with a slider plus Reset, and admittedly able to "temporarily exceed the limit before regular cleanup"; a smaller cap costs smoothness and raises data use. Desktop can relocate the cache; mobile cannot. (b) Downloads: durable and separate - clearing cache does not remove them. iOS stores them encrypted and may purge unused data itself; Android can move them to an SD card (1GB free, few minutes, app restarts). OFFLINE BACKUP is derived from pool (a), not a download: built from tracks recent playback left in cache, so it is gated on recent listening, free storage, an intact cache and offline listening enabled - clearing cache or reinstalling destroys it. Mobile sort order becomes the playback order in Android Auto and CarPlay; filters reach Android Auto but not CarPlay. QUALITY is three independent settings - Wi-Fi streaming, cellular streaming, downloads - over Low ~24kbps, Normal ~96, High ~160, Very high ~320, Lossless up to 24-bit/44.1kHz FLAC, plus an Auto-adjust toggle. Lossless must be enabled manually per device, wants a steady 1.5-2 Mbps, needs mobile 9.0.58+/desktop 1.2.67+, cannot cross Bluetooth, and is excluded on music videos, podcasts and audiobooks. NETWORK GATES: downloads default to Wi-Fi only ("Downloads over cellular" off); Data Saver cuts images and audio quality with no published bitrate; video podcasts download audio-only. Lyrics bundle into track downloads (Feb 2026). iOS gained background downloads (May 2026). Remote downloads are pushed to other account devices from the phone only.

## Spotify weaknesses

- **No single-song downloads** — The download unit is an album, playlist or show. Spotify states outright: "You can't download individual songs, but you can put them in a playlist and download the playlist." Wanting one track offline forces the user to manufacture a playlist, which then pollutes their library and their own playlist list. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/listen-offline/)
- **Spotify's own pages contradict each other on the download limit** — listen-offline says "You can download as many as 10,000 tracks on each of up to 5 different devices" (implying 50,000 account-wide), while your-premium-benefits says "A maximum of 10,000 tracks can be downloaded across up to 5 devices". A paying user cannot determine their own quota from the documentation. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/your-premium-benefits/)
- **30-day kill switch deletes local media** — "You need to go online at least once every 30 days to keep your downloads." Miss the window - a long deployment, a hospital stay, travel without data - and the files are deleted, not merely paused, and must be fully re-downloaded. Nothing is published about grace periods, partial expiry or warnings. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/listen-offline/)
- **Device-limit eviction is silent and retroactive** — Registering downloads on a 6th device strips "the device you've used least recently". There is no documented device list, no prompt naming which device will lose its downloads, and no way to pin a device - the user discovers the loss when that device next plays nothing. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/listen-offline/)
- **Downloads are opaque ciphertext the user cannot inspect or keep** — "On iPhone, Spotify saves downloads in an encrypted form on your device's storage." No published on-disk path for desktop downloads, no file access, no export, and nothing survives losing the subscription. The user pays for storage they cannot audit. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/storage-information/)
- **No storage accounting for downloads** — The only figure Spotify publishes is "We recommend having at least 1GB of free memory". There is no size per track or per quality tier, no per-playlist footprint, and the current storage article offers no bulk removal - only "manually removing downloaded tracks". Users cannot plan a trip's storage budget. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/storage-and-data-information/)
- **Cache is a soft cap that admits overrun, and the slider is barely shipped** — The docs concede the cache "may temporarily exceed the limit before regular cleanup" and publish no GB default. The mobile cache-limit slider is new - a September 2026 Android teardown reported it as "not yet live" - and no slider exists on desktop at all, where caches have long grown unbounded. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/storage-information/)
- **Data Saver is a single opaque switch** — "Data Saver reduces the app's data usage by showing fewer images and reducing audio quality" - with no stated bitrate, no MB-per-hour figures anywhere on the internet-and-data-usage page, and no separate control for images versus audio. The user cannot predict the data or quality cost of enabling it. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/internet-and-data-usage/)
- **Background downloading arrived on iOS only in May 2026** — Spotify announced "background downloads on iOS" on 28 May 2026, so until then an iPhone download required the app open and foregrounded. The announcement names no other platform and press coverage disagrees about Android's status, leaving the Android behaviour undocumented. (https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-05-28/playlist-folders-mobile-queue-controls-updates/)
- **Offline Backup is undocumented and fragile** — Spotify's own page will not say how the content is chosen, how much is stored or when it refreshes, and lists no off switch. It breaks if the cache is cleared, the app is reinstalled, storage runs low or the user has not listened recently - the safety net disappears exactly when storage is tight. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/offline-backup/)
- **Lossless cannot be retrofitted onto existing downloads** — Lossless is excluded on music videos, podcasts and audiobooks, must be enabled manually on each device, and is still "beginning to rollout across markets". Reported but unconfirmed: a "Bulk Downloads" feature to upgrade existing downloads to lossless - meaning today a user re-downloads everything by hand. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/lossless-audio-quality/)
- **A paid Spotify tier shipped with no offline at all** — Premium Lite, launched November 2025 in India, Indonesia, the UAE, Saudi Arabia and South Africa, was ad-free streaming up to 160kbps with offline listening excluded (reported); Spotify's Lite support pages now 404, consistent with the reported discontinuation. Paying Spotify has not always meant getting downloads. (https://www.billboard.com/pro/spotify-premium-platinum-subscription-plan-five-markets/)

## Unverifiable

- Whether the 30-day clock is per device or per account, and whether downloads re-download automatically on reconnect. Only community.spotify.com posts describe this; no Spotify-owned page states it.
- Whether Offline Mode shows a days-remaining countdown before the 30-day expiry. Community report only.
- The true account-wide download ceiling (10,000 or 50,000). Two Spotify-owned pages state it incompatibly; the 50,000 figure appears only in a community moderator post.
- What bitrate or tier Data Saver forces. Spotify says only that it reduces audio quality; no page gives a number.
- The codec used for mobile and desktop downloads. Spotify names only FLAC for Lossless and AAC (128/256kbit/s) for the web player; Ogg Vorbis is not stated on any page I could source.
- Where desktop stores downloaded (as opposed to cached) media on disk. Only the cache has a documented, relocatable location.
- Whether downloaded audiobooks are subject to the 5-device limit or the 30-day re-verification. The audiobook page states no device or expiry limit and the offline article omits audiobooks.
- Whether offline audiobook listening draws down the monthly Premium audiobook hours allocation. Neither the audiobook nor the benefits page addresses downloads against hours.
- Whether the mobile cache-limit slider has fully rolled out. The support page documents it; a September 2026 APK teardown (Spotify Android 9.1.88.815) reported it as not yet live.
- Whether Android has background downloads. The May 2026 newsroom post names iOS only, and press coverage conflicts on Android's prior behaviour.
