## Mechanics notes

CONNECT = LOCAL DISCOVERY + CLOUD CONTROL. Hardware advertises DNS-SD `_spotify-connect._tcp`; SRV gives IP+port, TXT gives a `CPath` key with the ZeroConf HTTP path (CPath=/zeroconf). Controller GETs action=getInfo and receives deviceID, deviceType, remoteName, publicKey, brand/modelDisplayName, libraryVersion, groupStatus, tokenType, clientID, productID, scope, availability, plus optional aliases, supported_drm_media_formats, supported_capabilities. Login is passwordless: POST action=addUser with userName, blob (encrypted creds), clientKey (decryption key), tokenType. Device must return InvalidArguments (303) without calling login if any is missing; call SpConnectionLoginZeroConf(); log out any prior user with SpConnectionLogout() and delete their data first; wait for the logged-in event before replying; never persist blob or clientKey, only creds from the new-credentials callback. Status is a JSON field (101 Ok, 102 Bad, 202 LoginFailed, 301/302/303 action errors, 402 SpotifyError) and HTTP 200 may be returned for all. resetUsers wipes to factory. Aliases make one box several virtual devices (multiroom zones): remoteName goes empty, names come from aliases, SpCallbackSelectedDeviceAliasChanged carries device_alias_index. An inactive device must stop mDNS, not just its HTTP endpoints. Discovery is local but the session is an account login, so logging into the device directly makes it visible off your Wi-Fi; first pairing needs same Wi-Fi. Audio streams from Spotify's servers to the device; the phone is only a remote, hence no Bluetooth pairing. CLOUD MIRROR: GET /me/player/devices (scope user-read-playback-state) returns id (unique, mostly persistent, not guaranteed, refetch periodically), is_active, is_private_session, is_restricted (accepts no API commands), name, type, volume_percent, supports_volume. PUT /me/player takes device_ids but exactly one id (more = 400) plus optional play:true; scope user-modify-playback-state; Premium only. OFFLINE: max 5 devices hold downloads, 10,000 tracks each; exceeding 5 strips the least-recently-used device; must go online once per 30 days. Offline Backup differs: a Premium playlist assembled from what is already in the device cache, no download step, on phone/Android Auto/CarPlay, unsortable from the car display. LOSSLESS: up to 24-bit/44.1 kHz FLAC, Premium, 50+ markets, ~1.5-2 Mbps steady; enabled per device and per Wi-Fi/cellular/downloads; excludes video, podcasts, audiobooks; Bluetooth re-compresses it.

## Spotify weaknesses

- **One stream per account, and no official page says so** — Starting playback on a second device pauses the first. Despite being one of the most-asked questions about Spotify, no official support article stating this surfaced in search; the rule is only documented in Community answers, which attribute it to personal-use licensing. Family plans do not change it. Staff replies suggest forcing playlists offline as the workaround. (https://community.spotify.com/t5/Accounts/Why-can-t-we-play-on-more-than-one-device/m-p/789185/highlight/true)
- **iOS hardware volume buttons cannot control a Connect device** — Spotify states that Apple discontinued the technology that let Spotify control connected devices' volume with the hardware buttons. Listeners must instead use an on-screen volume slider that appears when a volume button is pressed, or open it from the Connect menu in the Now Playing bar. A basic physical affordance is simply gone on iOS. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/spotify-connect/)
- **Bluetooth cannot carry Lossless, so the headline feature degrades on the most common link** — Spotify states plainly that Bluetooth does not provide enough bandwidth to transmit lossless audio, so the signal is re-compressed before being sent. Since most listeners use Bluetooth headphones or car audio, the FLAC path only pays off over Connect or wired. Spotify's own support page is vaguer, saying only that some connection methods may not support lossless. (https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-09-10/lossless-listening-arrives-on-spotify-premium-with-a-richer-more-detailed-listening-experience/)
- **Lossless must be turned on manually per device and per network type** — Spotify requires enabling Lossless manually on each device, and separately for Wi-Fi streaming, cellular streaming and downloads. Third-party Connect devices can only be switched from a mobile device via the Connect picker, and multi-room devices use yet another path. There is no account-level "always use the best quality available" setting. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/lossless-audio-quality/)
- **Playback may need reconnecting after a 10-minute pause** — Spotify documents that if you pause for more than 10 minutes you might need to reconnect to the Connect device. A paused session is not durably held, so stepping away from a speaker for a coffee can cost you the handoff. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/spotify-connect/)
- **Connect has no real access control: strangers' devices show up on shared networks** — Spotify's own troubleshooting acknowledges that devices belonging to other users on a shared network may appear in your picker, and that unfamiliar devices should be removed by logging them out, unlinking third-party apps such as Alexa, signing out everywhere and resetting your password. The remedies are account-level panic buttons, not per-device authorization. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/spotify-connect/)
- **Spotify deprecates Connect on older hardware and leaves the fix to the manufacturer** — Devices showing "No longer works with Spotify" have had built-in Connect support ended because, Spotify says, older devices use outdated technology that no longer meets our security standards. The suggested alternatives are Bluetooth, AirPlay or an audio cable, and Spotify concedes a firmware update may not exist for every model. Owners of working hardware lose the integration. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/connect-no-longer-supported/)
- **Car Thing was sold, discontinued, then remotely bricked** — Spotify's first hardware product was pulled from sale within months, and on 9 December 2024 remaining units were disabled; powering one on shows only that it is discontinued and no longer operational. Owners were told to reset and e-waste them. Refund coverage conflicted. The precedent matters: a cloud-dependent player can be switched off by its vendor. (https://9to5google.com/2024/12/09/spotify-officially-kills-car-thing-remaining-units-no-longer-work/)
- **Google Cast devices vanished from Connect on Android and the bug went unfixed** — Reported May 2026: Chromecast speakers, Chromecast Audio units and Nest speakers disappeared from Spotify Connect on Android, leaving a blank Connect menu, while Tidal, YouTube Music and Plex still saw them. Spotify confirmed the bug and said it was being looked into, with no timeline or root cause. The only reliable workaround was sideloading older APKs. (https://www.androidauthority.com/google-cast-devices-disappearing-from-spotify-connect-3665673/)
- **The Web API can only transfer to one device, and only for Premium** — PUT /me/player accepts a device_ids array but rejects more than one id with 400 Bad Request, so there is no programmatic grouped or multi-device playback. Control is Premium-only. Devices can also set is_restricted, meaning they accept no Web API commands at all, and the docs state some device models never appear in the device list. (https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/reference/transfer-a-users-playback)
- **Web player is the weakest client: no downloads, no Lossless, crippled Connect** — The listen-offline page does not list the web player among download-capable platforms. The Connect page states that on the web player you can only use devices you have already logged into, or Google Cast devices on your network, and Cast works in Chrome only. Lossless is absent from the page's platform list. Playback also depends on a Widevine DRM module. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/spotify-connect/)
- **Wearable and car integrations are uneven and arrive late** — Wear OS offline downloads only rolled out in August 2021, years after the watch app, after Spotify had to correct its own messaging about whether the feature existed. The Wear OS page still says features vary by watch model and does not cover Apple Watch at all. The new native car app launched on one manufacturer's lineup only and may require an Audi connect package. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/spotify-on-wear-os/)

## Unverifiable

- Apple Watch offline figures ("at least 10 hours of offline content", "100 tracks per playlist download cap") appear only in Digital Music News, not on any Spotify page. Spotify's own Apple Watch device page returned no readable content.
- Whether Spotify's Android Automotive OS / Google built-in app supports true offline downloads. Volvo and Polestar owners say in community posts that it only streams; one 2026 commercial blog claims downloads work. Spotify's own docs are silent.
- The web player's bitrate cap. Reports conflict: 128 kbps Free / 256 kbps Premium (BGR) versus 160 kbps Premium (community). No Spotify page states a figure.
- Whether "Hey Spotify" voice control still exists. A search summary of a Spotify support page mentions it, but the spotify-in-the-car page I fetched does not mention voice control at all.
- Whether Car mode is iOS-only, how it behaves on Android, and whether it still exists. The fetched spotify-in-the-car page did not mention Car mode at all; the claim rests on a summary of a possibly older page revision.
- The maximum number of participants in a Jam. The support article states no limit.
- Whether Lossless actually shipped to Sonos and Amazon devices. The September 2025 newsroom post promised them "next month"; no confirmation found.
- Whether Apple TV music videos are now live in the US and Canada. Press reports directly contradict each other, and no Spotify newsroom post for the October 2025 tvOS redesign was found.
- Whether multiple simultaneous Spotify streams genuinely work through Sonos via the partner API. One user claims yes; a Community reply says "I think" and admits never testing it.
- Spotify's Android Auto support page documents no features at all: it only lists Android Auto as a connection route and links out to Google, so Android Auto's actual capabilities and limits are not established from Spotify's own docs.
