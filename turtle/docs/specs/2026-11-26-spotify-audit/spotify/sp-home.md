## Mechanics notes

HOME RANKING. Home is shelves of cards. The deployed system (March 2025) uses a contextual bandit to predict a target content-type mix (music/podcast/audiobook) per request from context = time of day, day of week, device type, user+content embeddings; epsilon-greedy explore/exploit. The slate is built greedily from an empty set, each added item trading relevance against a KL-divergence penalty from the predicted mix. Reward = short-term stream-from-Home. Online: podcast i2s +36.6%, overall i2s +3.93%, minutes +1.28%, Home activity +1.54%. Predecessor BaRT (RecSys 2018): epsilon-greedy ranking of cards in a shelf, then shelves, plus "recsplanations" subtitles. CADENCES (support/find-playlists). Discover Weekly: Mondays. Release Radar: Fridays. On Repeat + Repeat Rewind: every 5 days. daylist: daily, more often the more you listen. Mixes and Your Daily Podcasts: no fixed schedule, "more often the more you listen". Blend: daily, up to 10 invitees. Niche Mixes: carousel of 5-10, refreshed daily, from "tens of thousands". RELEASE RADAR (artists help): max one song per artist per listener per week; a song persists up to 4 weeks if unplayed; already-played songs excluded (a sibling track may substitute); excludes Various Artists, re-releases, acoustic/karaoke/live alternates; remixes included; main/featured credit only; ordered by release date + predicted enjoyment, mostly stable weekly; pitch and deliver >=7 days ahead to influence selection. ON REPEAT: 30-day heavy-rotation window; mutually exclusive with Repeat Rewind at any instant. SHUFFLE: Premium picks "Standard" (uniform random, no memory of recent plays) or "Fewer repeats" (tracks recent plays, scores several candidate random sequences by a freshness score, keeps the best). Smart Shuffle injects recommendations into the session queue only, badged "Enhance" by the artist name; Premium disables it at Settings > Playback > "Include Smart Shuffle in play modes"; always on for Free mobile. PROMPTED PLAYLIST: prompt + full listening history since signup + real-time trends/charts/culture/history; user sets daily or weekly auto-refresh; one-line per-track rationale (Notes toggle); Refine vs Edit prompt; shared links regenerate per recipient for Premium in test markets; usage limits cap scheduled refreshes. TASTE PROFILE: free-text notes soft-steer Home, editable/deletable, visible in a few hours. Exclude-from-taste-profile drops a playlist's past and future plays from the profile but keeps it on Home.

## Spotify weaknesses

- **Smart Shuffle is forced on for Free listeners on mobile** — Spotify's own support page states "Smart Shuffle is always active on mobile" for free listeners, and the off-switch (Settings and privacy > Playback > "Include Smart Shuffle in play modes") is documented only for Premium. A whole tier cannot stop Spotify injecting recommendations into their own saved playlists. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/smart-shuffle/)
- **Home's removal controls are cosmetic by Spotify's own admission** — The Nov 2026 Home update says removing an item from a section "does not affect future recommendations" — it only tidies the feed. So the most discoverable Home control (press and hold > Remove from this section) teaches the model nothing; only "Not interested" and Taste Profile do. (https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-11-12/home-feed-updates/)
- **No shelf-level control of Home at all** — The Nov 2026 post enumerates exactly three Home controls, all item-level: remove a top tile, remove from Recents, and "Not interested". Nothing documented lets a listener reorder, pin, collapse or permanently hide an entire shelf, or set how much of Home is podcasts vs music — that mix is chosen for them by the calibration bandit. (https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-11-12/home-feed-updates/)
- **Taste Profile, the only steerable control, is Premium / 18+ / two markets / mobile** — The headline 2026 personalization feature is a beta limited to Premium listeners aged 18+ in the US and New Zealand, on mobile only. Its notes are explicitly "gentle guidance" with no guarantee, and changes take "typically" a few hours. Spotify does not say how notes are weighed against listening history. (https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-09-23/taste-profile-shape-home-feed/)
- **Prompted Playlist: Premium, mobile, English, ~7 markets, undisclosed usage limits** — Still beta nearly a year after the US/Canada launch. Creation is "For: Premium on mobile", "Currently only available in English", confirmed in roughly seven markets, and the support page states usage limits apply that also cap the daily/weekly auto-refresh — without publishing the numbers. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/prompted-playlists/)
- **DJ is Premium-only, still beta after three years, with no documented desktop or web support** — Announced in 2023 and still labelled beta in May 2026. Premium only, six languages, market-gated. Spotify's own DJ support page refuses to list markets (only "a limited number of languages") and documents only mobile menus, so desktop and web listeners have no documented path to it. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/dj/)
- **Discovery Mode puts paid commercial weighting inside Radio, Autoplay and every Spotify Mix** — Spotify names exactly three "Discovery Mode contexts" where artists pay a commission on streams in exchange for algorithmic promotion: Spotify Radio, Autoplay, and Spotify Mixes including Daily, Artist, Mood, Decade and Genre Mixes. Three of the most-used personalized surfaces are partly a paid-placement market, and the listener is not told. (https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/discovery-mode-contexts/)
- **Mixes and Your Daily Podcasts have no published refresh schedule** — Spotify will only say mixes "update more often the more you listen". Unlike Discover Weekly (Mondays) or Release Radar (Fridays), a listener cannot know when their Daily Mix or Your Daily Podcasts will change, cannot force a refresh, and cannot tell a stale mix from a deliberate one. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/spotify-mixes/)
- **Discover Weekly's steering controls are Premium-mobile only** — The 2025 redesign's genre controls — up to five personalized genre taps that each regenerate 30 tracks — rolled out to Premium on mobile, with expansion "to more listeners" only promised. Free listeners and desktop users get the unsteerable weekly playlist. (https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-06-30/discover-weekly-turns-10-celebrating-100-billion-tracks-streamed-and-a-decade-of-personalized-discovery/)
- **Spotify's own field experiment measured engagement bought with less individual diversity** — Spotify Research's "The Engagement-Diversity Connection" field experiment on podcast recommendations found the treatment decreased average individual-level diversity of podcast streams by 11.51% while increasing aggregate diversity by 5.96%. Optimising for short-term streams narrows each listener. (https://research.atspotify.com/publications/the-engagement-diversity-connection-evidence-from-a-field-experiment-on-spotify)
- **Recommendation surfaces are flooded by AI slop faster than detection scales** — Spotify said in Oct 2026 it kept 50m+ AI "slop and spam" tracks out of recommendations during 2026 alone, acknowledging no single signal catches them and that detection will get harder. The tracks stay on the platform, AI disclosure is not mandatory for all machine-assisted music, and press notes top AI artists still earned millions. (https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/spotify-has-pulled-more-than-50m-ai-slop-and-spam-tracks-out-of-its-recommendations-this-year/)
- **Free-tier on-demand play is capped by an allowance Spotify refuses to publish** — Spotify lifted shuffle-only playback for free users in 2025, but a spokesperson said each user has "a daily allocated amount of on-demand time", after which they fall back to six skips per hour; the company declined to state the figure. Queuing stays Premium, so a free listener cannot know when a personalized playlist stops being playable on demand. (https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/spotify-scraps-shuffle-restriction-for-users-of-free-tier/)

## Unverifiable

- Home filter chips and hero cards: no Spotify page describes an All/Music/Podcasts chip row or hero card on Home. A "Podcasts filter at the top of Home" is press-reported only (TechRadar, May 2025). Sept 2025 "Smart Filters" are Library filters.
- A "Your top mixes" shelf: no source, Spotify-owned or otherwise, confirms this shelf name exists. The Mixes support page never uses it. Treat it as a remembered label, not a real surface.
- Time Capsule: no current Spotify-owned page documents it. Absent from the Mixes page, the find-playlists table and the playlist-types page. Press calls it a past campaign superseded by On Repeat / Repeat Rewind. Do not present it as live.
- Spotify Stations as a live surface: the standalone Stations app shut down on 16 May 2022 (press plus a Spotify community FAQ) with users migrated to in-app Radio. No current Spotify-owned page documents a "Stations" surface in the main app.
- DJ's market list and current count: 75+ is documented (7 May 2026); 95+ is press-reported only (10 Nov 2026). The DJ support page lists no markets or languages. That Nico and Fabi replace the earlier Spanish voice Livi is reported, not documented.
- How many Daily Mixes a listener gets, and how Daily Mixes and Home card subtitles are named. The Mixes page gives no count and no naming rule; only patterns like "Feel Good Morning Mix". daylist's "thousands of descriptors" quote is third-party.
- Standard Discover Weekly track count and candidate pool size. Only the 30-track output of the 2025 genre controls is documented; the base playlist's length, pool size and how editor-chosen pools feed it are not published anywhere.
