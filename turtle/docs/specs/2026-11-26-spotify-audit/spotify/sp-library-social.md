## Mechanics notes

Jam: Premium needed to start/host and to join remotely; Free users join in person only ("Free users can join and add songs to a Jam hosted by a Premium user, allowing for in-person Jams"). Host toggles guest control of volume and of what's playing separately; with both off, guests can only add songs. If the host leaves, the Jam ends for everyone - no documented handoff. Join paths: QR code, share link, phones within 1m over Bluetooth, and automatic invites to users on the same Wi-Fi. In-person Jams also run on TV, Android Auto (14.5+, Spotify 9.0.50+) and CarPlay, playing on those speakers. Blend: up to 10 invited friends; refreshes daily from all participants' listening; artist Blends joined by invite link from the artist's socials or Artist pick. Members see each other's profile picture and username. Collaborative playlists: invite link valid 7 days; collaborators add, remove, reorder; avatars under the title manage membership. Wrapped 2025: window 1 Jan to mid-November, shipped 3 Dec; a listen counts after >30s; primary artists weighted above featured; eligibility is >=30 songs each played >30s plus 5 distinct artists; Private Mode plays and taste-excluded tracks are excluded; one stream is one listen regardless of length; offline plays included. Listening Stats: rolling last four weeks of top artists and songs plus one weekly highlight (milestone, new discovery or fan moment), from the profile menu; 60+ markets; Free and Premium. Private Session: account-level, applies to every device, auto-ends after 6 hours or on app restart; listening is then excluded from Listening Activity, profile recently-played, recommendations and Wrapped. Listening Activity: opt-in under Privacy and social settings; shows current or most recent track to connected Messages contacts only; six emoji reactions; since 2026-06-17 it reaches group chats and is shared with a group by default on joining (changeable per group); since 2026-09-17 a user's activity can be hidden without unfollowing. Request to Jam invites time out in a few minutes; the accepting recipient becomes host. Library ordering: filters combine, sort/filter choices persist between sessions, and playlist "Custom order" can only be set on desktop but shows on all devices. Durability (inferred, server-mediated): deleted playlists recoverable 90 days via a web page; downloads need an online check every 30 days, capped at 10,000 tracks on each of up to 5 devices, music downloads Premium-only.

## Spotify weaknesses

- **No native library or playlist export; only a privacy data request** — Import is a first-class, in-app flow via TuneMyMusic (newsroom 2025-11-20), but the only documented export is a privacy ZIP requested from the Account Privacy page or by emailing privacy@spotify.com. The page does not even state what it contains or how long it takes, so getting your own library out is a support process, not a feature. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/data-rights-and-privacy-settings/)
- **"Make this a playlist" is a dead snapshot; no rule-based playlists** — Filtering Liked Songs and tapping "Make this a playlist" copies the current set once: "New items you've liked are not automatically added to the playlist." Nothing in Spotify's playlist documentation describes a user-defined rule that keeps a playlist up to date, so every saved view goes stale the moment you like something new. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/your-library/)
- **Roughly 10,000 items per playlist, and the number is not documented** — No Spotify support page states a playlist cap. Community moderators give about 10,000 items and say it cannot be raised, while another says there is no hard cap but the app may misbehave near it; users report a "filled to the max" error near 9,997. A hard product limit that only forum staff describe, inconsistently, is a documentation failure as well as a ceiling. (https://community.spotify.com/t5/Your-Library/Is-There-a-Limit-To-How-Many-Songs-Are-on-a-Playlist/td-p/5462421)
- **Smart Filters are Premium, seven markets, mobile and tablet only** — The one feature that organises a library by activity, mood or genre launched "for Premium users in the coming weeks" on mobile and tablet in Australia, Canada, Ireland, New Zealand, South Africa, the UK and the US. Most of Spotify's listeners, and every desktop user, get only name, date and creator sorting. (https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-09-05/new-user-controls-personalize-listening/)
- **Social presence is gated by age, market and platform** — Messages, and therefore Listening Activity and Request to Jam, are mobile-only, for users 16 and older, in select markets. Spotify's modern social layer is not something a given listener can assume they have; the older desktop Friend Activity remains the fallback. (https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-08-26/introducing-messages-a-new-way-to-share-what-you-love-on-spotify-with-friends-and-family/)
- **Jam needs Premium on both ends to work remotely, and dies with the host** — "Premium is needed to start and host a Jam" and "You need Premium to join a Jam remotely"; Free users are confined to in-person sessions on one speaker. "If a host leaves, it'll end the Jam for everyone", with no documented handoff to another participant, so one person's dropped connection ends the session. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/jam/)
- **Wrapped stops counting in mid-November and silently drops listening** — "Wrapped captures your listening from January 1 until mid November", so two to six weeks of the year never appear. Private Mode streams and taste-excluded tracks are omitted, and a personalised Wrapped requires at least 30 songs played over 30 seconds each plus five different artists, so light listeners get nothing. (https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-12-05/wrapped-methodology-explained/)
- **The only in-app stats surface covers four weeks, in 60+ markets** — Listening Stats shows "top artists and songs from the past four weeks" and is limited to "more than 60 markets worldwide". Between a rolling four-week window and one annual Wrapped there is no documented monthly, seasonal or all-time view of your own listening, and no per-track play counts. (https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-11-06/spotify-new-feature-listening-stats/)
- **Core controls are split arbitrarily across platforms** — Playlist "Custom order" can only be set in the desktop app although it "shows on all devices"; removing a follower is mobile-only while blocking works on both. A listener cannot learn one platform and expect the same controls elsewhere. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/sort-and-filter/)
- **A deleted playlist is gone after 90 days, via a web page** — "If you deleted a playlist within the last 90 days, you can get it back" - and recovery runs through a separate web page rather than the app. There is no documented version history, no undo inside a playlist, and no recovery path stated for Liked Songs or followed playlists. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/save-recover-playlists/)
- **Everything is leased from Spotify's servers (inferred)** — Downloads are Premium-only for music, capped at "as many as 10,000 tracks on each of up to 5 different devices", and expire unless "you go online at least once every 30 days". Library, playlists, collaboration, stats, Blend refreshes and recovery all live server-side, so a lapsed subscription or an offline month ends access to a collection the listener organised. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/listen-offline/)
- **The documentation itself is undated and self-contradictory** — The playlist privacy page says "Shared links will expire after 7 days" while also saying a private playlist cannot be opened "even if they have a link to it". Support articles carry no revision date (only a 2026 Spotify AB copyright), several describe menus that no longer match the app, and hard limits such as pin counts and playlist caps appear only in forum posts. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/playlist-privacy-and-access/)

## Unverifiable

- Maximum number of Jam participants. Press from 2023 cited 32; a Spotify Community reply says "There isn't a fixed number as of right now" and that the feature is not designed for large numbers. No support page states a cap.
- Whether a Free user can accept a Request to Jam. The newsroom post says the accepting recipient becomes the Jam host, but the Jam support page says only Premium can host. Nothing resolves the conflict.
- Any cap on the number of collaborators on a collaborative playlist. The support article does not mention one.
- A 10,000-playlists-per-account ceiling. This rests on a single Spotify Community moderator reply roughly 1,300 days old, with no newer or official confirmation.
- Whether Liked Songs itself has a size cap. One community staff reply says there is no limit; a 2020 press report says the library limit was removed but the 10,000 cap still applies to individual playlists.
- Whether Blend shows a numeric taste-match score, and how any such percentage is computed. The Blend support page mentions only seeing "how your music tastes match" for artist Blends.
- Whether drag-to-reorder on mobile is Premium-only. This appeared only in a search snippet attributed to a Spotify page; the collaborative-playlist and create-playlist articles state no tier.
- Contents and turnaround of the "Download your data" ZIP: claims of 90 days of searches, a Playlist.json covering all saved playlists, roughly a year of history, and waits of 5 to 30 days all come from community forum posts, not a Spotify page.
- Spotify Wrapped 2026. As of 24 November 2026 no Spotify announcement of a launch date was found; the widely circulated 2 December 2026 date is an Official Charts prediction, not a Spotify statement.
- Whether Summer Rewind still runs, and the current On Repeat / Repeat Rewind refresh cadence. The newest primary Summer Rewind source is 2020, and the five-day cadence traces to 2019 material plus a support table.
