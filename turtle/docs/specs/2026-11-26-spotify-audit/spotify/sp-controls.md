## Mechanics notes

TWO DISTINCT "TASTE PROFILES" — do not conflate. (1) The internal profile Exclude acts on: feeds Home, Search, Discover Weekly, Radio and Blend/Wrapped summaries; Free and Premium, global. (2) Taste Profile (Beta): a natural-language editor, Premium + mobile + 18+, English/Spanish, affects Home ONLY and explicitly not Wrapped, cannot set permanent always/never rules. EXCLUSION MECHANICS (engineering.atspotify.com, Oct 2023 — playlist exclusion only; it states tracks could not then be excluded, which Oct 2025 changed). The filter is applied inside the shared taste-profile systems, not per surface, so consuming surfaces needed no changes. Profile inputs named: recent listens, top tracks and artists, user embeddings (the user's position in an "abstract musical universe"), and in-app interactions. The A/B test first filtered only embeddings and On Repeat; production later added recent listening, top tracks/artists and interactions. Critically, Home Shortcuts and Recently Played deliberately read an UNFILTERED view, being retrieval rather than recommendation surfaces. CADENCE. Exclusions and the kids/family toggle apply within 48 hours; weekly surfaces such as Discover Weekly may lag until the following week. Taste Profile (Beta) notes apply in minutes. Snooze has a fixed 30-day expiry. Exclusion covers past AND future plays, retroactively rewriting the profile rather than only gating new plays. SCOPE is context-bound, keyed to the play's context rather than track identity: a stream of the same song from Liked Songs still counts. The blog also notes the system does not infer genres from an excluded playlist. ADOPTION. Author's own listening: >30% sleep music. A/B test: ~12% of targeted users opened the menu, across ~100 editorial playlists. Post-launch Feb 2023: >20M users; ~4% more recommendation listening; feature users consumed nearly 10% more music overall; UI in 88 languages. ORDERING includes non-satisfaction terms: content cost and monetization "may influence" picks, and Discovery Mode lets rightsholders buy a likelihood boost (commission-funded, excluded from editorial playlists, no guarantee). PRIVATE SESSION is account-level, propagates to TVs, speakers and Connect devices, expires after 6 hours or on restart, requires Listening activity on, and excludes the session from personalization and Wrapped. The export carries a private-session flag per play, so the exclusion is recorded per stream rather than by dropping the row.

## Spotify weaknesses

- **No selective listening-history deletion** — There is no documented way to clear Recents or delete individual plays. Spotify states that because it must process some personal data to provide the service, deleting that data requires closing the account. The in-app Recents article documents viewing only, with no clear or remove action. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/data-rights-and-privacy-settings/)
- **History view is capped and undefined** — Desktop Recents shows only "the last 50 songs". No cap or time window is documented for mobile. Recently played artists on the profile is all-or-nothing: individual artists cannot be removed, and "recently" is never defined. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/recent-activity/)
- **Hide is per-playlist and blocked on your own playlists** — Hiding suppresses a track inside one playlist only — "They can still be played in other playlists where you haven't hidden them." It works only on playlists made by Spotify or other users; for your own playlists Spotify tells you to delete the track instead. Hiding has no effect on the taste profile. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/hide-unhide-songs/)
- **The only cross-surface song suppression expires after 30 days** — "Don't suggest this song anywhere for 30 days" is the sole documented global suppression, is reached only through the mobile three-dot menu (no desktop path documented), is listed as a Premium control, and lapses automatically. There is no permanent never-recommend-this equivalent. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/hide-unhide-songs/)
- **Exclusion leaks through other contexts** — Excluding a playlist or track reduces influence only for plays in that context. Spotify documents that listening to the same track from outside the excluded playlist, such as from Liked Songs, still counts toward the taste profile — so the control is not a property of the song. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/exclude-playlists-or-tracks-from-your-taste-profile/)
- **Taste controls take up to 48 hours, and weekly playlists up to a week** — Both exclusion and the kids-and-family toggle take effect within 48 hours, and surfaces such as Discover Weekly may not reflect the change until the following week. A listener who corrects their profile cannot see the result in the session where they made the correction. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/exclude-playlists-or-tracks-from-your-taste-profile/)
- **The only window into the profile is heavily gated** — Taste Profile (Beta), the one surface showing Spotify's model of a listener, requires Premium, mobile and age 18+, is described elsewhere as available only "where available", and its AI summary exists in English and Spanish only — unsupported device languages silently default to English rather than showing nothing. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/your-taste-profile/)
- **Natural-language taste edits cannot express rules** — Taste Profile (Beta) explicitly cannot set permanent rules like always or never showing something, and cannot take actions such as following an artist. Durable preferences therefore have no home: free text is advisory, exclusion is retrospective, and snooze expires. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/your-taste-profile/)
- **No per-recommendation explanation, while paid influence is acknowledged** — Spotify's explainer gives only illustrative reasoning ("If you listen to a certain artist, we might recommend more songs by that artist") and documents no "why am I seeing this" on any item. It simultaneously admits monetization "may influence" recommendations and that Discovery Mode buys a likelihood boost — never disclosed on the affected item. (https://www.spotify.com/us/safetyandprivacy/understanding-recommendations)
- **No documented artist-level block** — Nothing in the In-app features article index, the follow-artists article, or the recommendation controls list documents blocking an artist or a "don't play this artist" control. Blocked users covers other listeners, not artists. Granularity available is track, playlist, or the kids-and-family category — never an artist. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/follow-artists-podcasts-and-shows/)
- **Onboarding personalization is undocumented** — Spotify's own Getting started article describes signup method and optional profile fields, then jumps straight to searching for something to play. It documents no artist or genre picker and no cold-start step, saying only that Spotify learns as you use it. A new listener has no documented way to seed or inspect their profile. (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/getting-started/)
- **No turnaround commitment for the data export** — No Spotify-owned page read here states how long a data request takes. The support articles on downloading and understanding your data give no timeline, and the privacy policy offers only a backward-looking 2025 "Average response rate" of 3.89 days for access requests — not a commitment, and not specific to the lifetime Extended streaming history package. (https://www.spotify.com/us/legal/privacy-policy/)

## Unverifiable

- The claim that Spotify signup asks new users to pick at least three artists. Spotify's own Getting started article documents no artist or genre picker; the only sources were an undated Medium post and a Mobbin capture 4-6 years old.
- Data export delivery time. Third-party guides state a 30-day maximum and 5-14 days in practice; no Spotify-owned page read here states any figure, so these are third-party at best and must not be called documented.
- The exact JSON keys of the Extended streaming history export. Spotify documents 21 field types in prose, but the authoritative schema is in the bundled "Read Me First" file inside the user's own ZIP, which is not publicly fetchable.
- Whether private-session plays still appear in Recents. The private-listening article says profile recently-played does not update; the data-rights page says Recently Played is hidden; Community posts report plays still showing. Unresolved.
- The default state of the "Include kids and family music in your taste profile" toggle. The support article documents the path and effects but never says whether it ships on or off.
- The strength, duration, decay and surface coverage of "Not interested" and thumbs down. Spotify states only that they produce fewer similar recommendations; no support article documents the controls, so their mechanics are unknown.
- Market availability for Taste Profile (Beta), Prompted Playlist prompt reuse and DJ. Spotify says "where available", "test markets" and "a limited number of languages" without naming any. The market_limited flags follow those hedges.
- Whether Taste Profile (Beta) notes influence anything beyond Home. The article says Home only and not Wrapped, but is silent on Search, Radio, Daily Mixes and Discover Weekly, so the blast radius is unknown.
- Whether any artist-level block exists in the shipping app. It is absent from every Spotify page read here; absence from documentation is not proof of absence from the product.
- Coverage limit: the shared WebSearch budget ran out after four queries, so the rest came from WebFetch of Spotify URLs found via the support site's own link graph. A Technobezz report on new Home hiding controls returned HTTP 403, so it is unread.
