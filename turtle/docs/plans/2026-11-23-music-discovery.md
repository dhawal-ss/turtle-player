# Better music discovery in Turtle

**Implementation handoff for Claude Opus 5.5 · 23 November 2026 · Proposed**

Give listeners two dependable ways into music. Home should offer something they would choose now. Explore should tell them what people in their country are playing. When they press play on either, the next few songs should make sense in the context they chose.

**Build first.** Verify a source for a weekly country songs chart. In parallel, improve the opening tracks of Matched shelves and Infinity sessions. Those fixes can ship even if chart acquisition falls through. Keep the existing taste engine and YouTube playback. Reconsider a learned ranker once real listening evidence shows which decisions the current engine gets wrong.

This updates W7 (country charts), the phone entry point from W6 (Explore), and W12 (evaluation). It pulls forward the parts of W4 and W11 that help people start listening. W1, W2, W3 and W9 are already present at the audited checkout, `3b1f09b1`, four commits ahead of its tracked branch. Recheck the implementation base before starting. The [22 November design](../specs/2026-11-22-personal-home-design.md) records the earlier product decisions.

## What to borrow from Spotify and Apple Music

- **Give charts a clear identity.** Spotify publishes daily and weekly chart windows and explains what counts towards rank. Apple Music puts Daily Top 100 playlists beside trending songs, releases and expert selections. Turtle should show the market, source and reporting period so a listener opening the app on Friday knows which week they are hearing. [Spotify chart methodology](https://support.spotify.com/vu-en/artists/article/understanding-spotify-charts/) · [Apple Music discovery guide](https://support.apple.com/en-euro/guide/iphone/iph2c41e6189/ios)
- **Choose the pool, then sequence the session.** Spotify Engineering describes editors choosing a pool and systems selecting and ordering tracks for a listener, with listening, saves and skips informing later choices. Turtle can use a verified chart or a chosen artist to define the pool, then apply on-device affinity and spacing to a personal mix. This is feasible without an editorial team. [Spotify Engineering on playlist sequencing](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2023/04/humans-machines-a-look-behind-spotifys-algotorial-playlists)
- **Give listeners a quick correction.** Apple asks for favorite artists and genres and offers Favorite and Suggest Less on the item. Spotify explains how feedback and exclusions affect recommendations. Turtle already has history controls, hidden items and playlist-add feedback. Make the correction available where the miss happens and show its effect on the next eligible suggestion. [Apple onboarding](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/get-personalized-recommendations-iph2b1748696/27/ios/27) · [Apple feedback](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/guide/iphone/iph744ea4009/ios) · [Spotify recommendation controls](https://www.spotify.com/uk/safetyandprivacy/understanding-recommendations)
- **Count the right thing.** YouTube’s weekly Top Songs aggregates eligible uses of a recording. Its music video, artist and Shorts charts measure different activity. The public Data API’s regional most-popular result ranks videos. Treat the official weekly songs issue as the reference for a songs chart. Spotify and Apple are research references, with no dependency on their catalogs or playback. [YouTube chart definitions](https://support.google.com/youtubemusic/answer/9014376?hl=en-GB) · [YouTube Data API](https://developers.google.com/youtube/data/reference/rest/v3/videos/list)

## The listener experience

**Home.** Keep the grid and Made for you where returning listeners expect them. Use the reserved W7 slot below personal content for “From the charts in [country]”. Show up to three playable picks with their real ranks and a “See chart” action that opens the same issue. For someone new to Turtle, use the first available content slot when personal shelves are thin. Let them play immediately or choose artists. A metal listener in India should be able to find the country’s number one in Explore while keeping a metal-focused Home.

**Explore.** Give phone users a visible entry from Home through the existing Shell navigation surfaces. Reuse the desktop destination. Lead with a short chart preview. The full list shows country, source and week, with a play action for the list and a playable row for each recording. If the listener hides No. 2, the next row still reads **3**. A “Rising” indicator requires two consecutive comparable issues. Show how many positions the recording moved.

**Into playback and back.** Tapping a chart song continues through the visible chart order. Tapping a personal shelf song continues in the personal order that was displayed. A mix card keeps its one-tap play action and gains a separate detail action for inspecting tracks. In Up Next, give suggested songs a short reason backed by the actual selection, such as “From your [artist] radio” or “No. 8 in [country] this week.” Preserve manually queued tracks. Returning from the player restores the same chart issue and scroll position.

**Finish.** Use Shell’s existing artwork, typography and now-playing treatment. Reserve artwork space before images load. Align ranks, allow long translated names and announce rank, song, artist and playback state together to screen readers. Make play, details, feedback and undo reachable by touch and keyboard. A chart refresh runs independently of first Home paint. Check a small phone, desktop keyboard navigation, reduced motion, large text and offline return.

### Country chart source and ranking contract

1. Follow a provider-linked browse or playlist through the existing [YouTube repository](../../../shared/src/commonMain/kotlin/com/metrolist/shared/api/YouTubeRepository.kt). Check the first ten ranks, artists and recording versions against the matching official issue in two markets. Vary content language independently and repeat signed in and signed out. Record coverage, mismatches, access conditions and a reduced permitted fixture without session identifiers. Public chart pages give a human a reference. An app-consumable response and permission for the intended use still need verification. [US weekly songs](https://charts.youtube.com/charts/TopSongs/us/weekly) · [India weekly songs](https://charts.youtube.com/charts/TopSongs/in/weekly)
2. Put I/O, caching and playable-item resolution in the bridge. Give the pure taste engine immutable evidence per recording. A snapshot carries source, chart kind, verified market, period, retrieval time, original ranks and provider recording IDs. Track resolution status separately. Prefer exact IDs. An alternate upload needs matching artist, normalized title and compatible version, using duration when available. Leave ambiguous covers, live takes and remixes unresolved. Preserve original ranks across filtering and unavailable tracks.
3. Reuse the resolved country from [GlobalYouTube](../../../shared/src/commonMain/kotlin/com/metrolist/shared/state/GlobalYouTube.kt). A selected content country takes precedence, and SYSTEM uses the system region. The UI language can differ. Avoid location permissions or a silent country fallback. Key issues by source, country, chart kind and period. Invalidate localized or entitlement-dependent projections when language or account changes. Coalesce simultaneous refreshes, commit only complete issues, and discard responses for a superseded market.
4. Start with weekly issues. Use the provider’s date on cached charts. Refresh in the background around publication with bounded retries. After a missed issue, keep the dated chart in Explore, mark it stale and suspend current-week claims on Home. Offer an empty state with access to the rest of Explore for unsupported countries. A verified global chart could be an explicit choice. Derive movement only from complete, comparable issues, and never use download time as the chart date.
5. Keep country evidence alongside the candidate so it expires. For the personal shelf, filter eligible tracks, rank by known taste and use source rank to break close ties. For ordinary radio and Infinity, ship candidate-quality fixes first. Trial a chart bonus only among candidates supported by the seed, chosen artist or session. Chart membership alone contributes no learned preference. Uncharted music stays eligible for discovery.
6. If the source cannot establish country, period and order, or support the intended usage, defer the ranked feature. Explore can still show provider suggestions under a descriptive label without rank numbers. Search order, lifetime views, Shorts rankings and scraped competitor charts cannot substantiate the claim. A licensed chart feed would require a separate cost and product decision.

## How to decide whether listening improves

**Baseline.** The current CI replay uses 30 synthetic listeners. It compares Turtle against popularity and random baselines for all finished tracks and for previously unheard tracks. Keep the existing 2% relative regression guard on recall and NDCG. Add a time-split replay on opt-in local histories or a redistribution-cleared fixture. Freeze candidates, chart issues and user state at each cutoff. Compare current Turtle, provider order, chart order and the proposed selection over the same eligible inputs. Report cold, light and established listeners separately. A replay cannot establish whether someone would enjoy a song they were never offered. [Replay evaluator](../../engines/taste/src/commonMain/kotlin/app/turtleplayer/taste/eval/ReplayEvaluator.kt) · [Golden test](../../engines/taste/src/commonTest/kotlin/app/turtleplayer/taste/OfflineReplayTest.kt)

| Measure | Definition and decision |
| --- | --- |
| Early recommendation skips | Among the first five suggested plays after a deliberate start, divide user skips under 30 seconds by eligible started suggestions. Exclude manual selections, errors and interrupted sessions. Report counts per listener, surface and profile maturity. [Existing play event semantics](../../engines/events/src/commonMain/kotlin/app/turtleplayer/events/PlayEvent.kt) |
| Discoveries worth keeping | Of distinct previously unheard suggested recordings played, count the share liked, added to a playlist or deliberately replayed within seven days. Allow the full follow-up period. Read alongside skip rate to catch an overly familiar queue. |
| Chart integrity | Compare displayed ranks and period with the source. Report playable mappings divided by source entries before user filtering, and inspect misses by market. A wrong country, fabricated rank, hidden-item leak or reordered manual queue blocks release. |
| Responsiveness and breadth | Compare time from Home open to chosen play, tap to audible playback and p95 time to usable Home on the same devices and networks. Count degraded or empty recommendation paths locally. Report unheard-track share and distinct artists per ten suggestions alongside latency. |

**Listening trial.** Run paired five-song sessions on the same seed, device and candidate snapshot. Randomize the order and hide the policy label. Include mainstream, niche and multilingual listeners across at least two markets. Ask “Would you keep listening?” and “Which transition lost you?” For misses, record the recording version, candidate source, reason and position. Include a country switch with the language held fixed, a mid-session dislike and a small eligible pool. Use the small panel to find defects. Before enabling reranking by default, seek lower early skips and stable or better discovery saves/replays in a larger opt-in comparison. Report uncertainty by listener. Keep the existing default when results are inconclusive or a segment materially worsens.

**Rollout and verification.** Land the baseline before tuning. Keep chart display and personal reranking independently reversible via Turtle configuration. Retain listening feedback and dated snapshots on rollback. Keep histories and diagnostic detail on device by default. Inject clocks and randomness for engine tests. Use reduced fixtures for parser and persistence contracts, and manual device checks for navigation, accessibility and actual playback. Run `./gradlew -p turtle/engines check`, affected bridge tests, and the Android and desktop builds in [repository checks](../../../docs/checks.md). The current [bridge](../../bridge/build.gradle.kts) and [UI](../../ui/build.gradle.kts) target Android and desktop. iOS Shell integration needs separate platform work. Run shared iOS checks if changing shared production code.

**Fork and ownership.** Use existing Shell slots and bridge seams. Aim for zero additional hooks. The [fork guide](../../AGENTS.md) and [hook ledger](../../HOOKS.md) quote different ceilings. Reconcile them if a later change genuinely requires an additional line. Preserve localized resources, desktop Home’s library/local exclusions, paused history, spoken-word separation and the existing playback backend. The fork’s issues are disabled and its PR listing was empty at review. Upstream merged classic recommendations and has an open proposal for suggestions on editable playlists. Keep that playlist work separate. Recheck assignments and related PRs before taking an implementation ticket. [Classic recommendations](https://github.com/MetrolistGroup/Metrolist/pull/4813) · [Editable-playlist suggestions](https://github.com/MetrolistGroup/Metrolist/pull/5190)

**First review from the implementation agent.** Deliver the two-market chart evidence and an acquisition decision. Bring one replay case each for a weak leading shelf item, a direct Infinity hit and a radio page with an unknown seed. The team can then independently assess the two claims listeners care about: “this is popular here” and “I want to hear the next song.”
