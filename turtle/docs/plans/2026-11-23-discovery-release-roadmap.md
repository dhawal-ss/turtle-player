# Turtle discovery and release roadmap

23 November 2026 · Review of `main` at `168e7104a` · Android and Windows launch scope

## Recommendation

Make Turtle the open-source player where you can find the right recording, hear a good next song, and control what the player learns. Match Spotify’s clarity about what each playlist is for. Beat the reference experience on explainability, private listening, and control over discovery. Measure that advantage on specific listening tasks before making a wider parity claim.

Start with the first five songs of a station, reliable learning signals, and mix details that open without replacing playback. Put Browse within one action on both platforms and give every collection a clear purpose. Ship unranked, accurately attributed popular picks while the ranked chart source is unresolved. Keep Turtle an open-source client for v1. Treat a licensed streaming service as a separate investment decision with a catalog, rights, operations, and budget of its own. [Product plan](../../../../Metrolist%20Fork%20Product%20and%20Engineering%20Plan.md) · [Current handoff](2026-11-23-status.md)

This plan starts after discovery steps 1–4b. Matched shelf ordering, qualified Infinity hits, station-seeded radio pages, and suggestion origin tags already exist. The opening desktop radio path, mix details, artist onboarding, and true charts are still open. The handoff is the source for completed work and prior device observations. ## P0 · Make playback and learning trustworthy

1. **Use one eligibility policy on every generated queue path.** Desktop’s initial `freshRadioRecommendations` bypasses Turtle’s ranker and Not interested handling. Android fallback branches can miss provider explicit/video filters, and a fully filtered page can stall continuation. Route initial and continuation pages through a shared bridge contract. Apply entitlement and content policy, then recording-level hides and duplicate checks, then ranking. Reapply eligibility on timeout, empty/single-item and provider-order fallbacks. Continue a bounded number of pages for sparse pools and expose a recoverable end state. Verify station B while A is playing, cold observer, a hidden alternate upload, blocked-only pages, timeout and user-edited queue on both platforms. [Desktop opening page](../../../desktopApp/src/jvmMain/kotlin/com/metrolist/desktop/state/playback/PlayerState.kt#L3747) · [Android queue](../../../androidApp/src/main/kotlin/com/metrolist/android/playback/AndroidQueueCoordinator.kt) · [Bridge ranking and fallback](../../bridge/src/commonMain/kotlin/app/turtleplayer/bridge/TurtleRuntime.kt#L215)
2. **Bind learning to a queue occurrence.** `rankExternal` reads session state, but production ranks provider pages and Infinity refills through separate paths. The engine’s in-memory queue lifecycle has no production caller, so its session reset and protected-slot behavior do not establish app behavior. An external play can also be counted as Infinity feedback via a stale song-ID entry. Carry occurrence, station/mix ID and session generation from request to impression, play and feedback. Reset short-term state on a new station or idle session, cap its memory, preserve user-added entries and the next two visible automatic entries, then rank beyond those boundaries or at refill. Reject stale async rankings by generation. Verify first-track attribution and never infer discovery feedback from a manual replay of the same recording. [Taste engine](../../engines/taste/src/commonMain/kotlin/app/turtleplayer/taste/TasteEngine.kt#L196) · [Infinity feedback](../../bridge/src/commonMain/kotlin/app/turtleplayer/bridge/InfinityMix.kt#L262) · [Runtime feedback](../../bridge/src/commonMain/kotlin/app/turtleplayer/bridge/TurtleRuntime.kt#L299)
3. **Fix privacy at the setting transition and repair old signal.** The tracker samples the pause flag at start and when recording or checkpointing. A mid-track pause followed by a resume can leave the whole play recordable. On pause, invalidate the in-progress event and pending checkpoint across both platforms, including crash recovery and background playback. Keep explicit votes separate and define whether they still apply in private listening. Preserve existing history and version event labeling. Use a reversible profile rebuild that discounts unreliable legacy skips, makes stopping, errors and queue replacement neutral, and offers an opt-in taste reset. The handoff reports 80 suspect observations among 128 quick skips on one phone. Use that case to test recovery and measure new builds independently. [Session tracker](../../engines/events/src/commonMain/kotlin/app/turtleplayer/events/ListeningSessionTracker.kt) · [Recorded phone observation](2026-11-23-status.md#known-issues)
4. **Treat mobile playback reliability as a release criterion.** Reproduce the reported Bluetooth and USB lock-screen stop with synthetic listening where practical. Record timestamps and redacted audio focus, media session and player transitions. Verify screen lock, queue end, network handoff, resume and manual edits. Add cross-platform smoke tests for a fresh launch, first play, seek, pause/resume, Bluetooth on Android and queue recovery. Keep experiments off on degraded builds and publish a rollback procedure. [Known car behavior](2026-11-23-status.md#known-issues)

## P2 · Improve recommendation quality in measured steps

Keep the current on-device ranker as the baseline. In the bridge, assemble eligible candidates from local affinity and saved music, current provider radio, followed releases, a tested open similarity source, and clearly labeled editorial or popular collections. Retain source, seed and confidence for each pick. In the engine, rank for the surface’s objective, then sequence for artist and recording spacing, controlled novelty and continuity. Cache and resolve playability in the bridge and keep clocks, randomization and candidate fixtures deterministic. Reuse Shell slots for presentation. [Engine seams and constraints](../../AGENTS.md) · [Spotify research on sequencing](https://research.atspotify.com/publications/exploiting-sequential-music-preferences-via-optimisation-based-sequencing)

| Surface | Ranking objective | First experiment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Familiar mixes | Reliable opening, recognizable artists and low repetition across the Home page | Score the first three visible tracks and apply distinct artists across neighboring mixes; compare against current Matched ordering on the same pool. |
| Weekly Discovery | New-to-listener recordings and artists with enough support to be plausible | Add an open similar-artist candidate source, cap repeated artists, and compare incremental playable candidates and later saves or returns. |
| Infinity and radio | Honor station intent and the current session, then vary the next songs | Compare first five transitions and refills with fixed seeds. Tune the effect of repeated skips and vary transitions within the listener-selected discovery range. |
| Fresh for You and Rediscover | Releases from followed artists and favorites that are due for a replay | Distinguish verified release date from first seen. Rotate release candidates across followed artists, and reset Rediscover cooldown from a real replay. |
| Browse and playlists | A coherent genre, mood or source-defined order | Keep authored and chart order intact. Offer a separate personal station or mix from the same pool where allowed. |

Use MusicBrainz core metadata and ListenBrainz open listen data for a small, reproducible similarity pilot. Measure join precision, playable coverage, novelty and regional representation before downloading a large pack. Ship only derived, minimized, signed and versioned artifacts with checksum, expiry, attribution, memory and disk limits, and a rollback to the previous pack. Rebuild periodically from a full source dump so removed listens do not persist solely through incremental updates. Keep raw personal history on device. Evaluate a learned ranker, on-device audio features and contextual bandits only after the pilot identifies an observed failure and demonstrates data and rights readiness. Keep the existing 2% relative recall and NDCG regression checks. The current synthetic evaluator uses 30 heavy listeners generated from the same genre structure as its vectors, calls the generic engine path, and divides recall by `min(K, holdout size)`. Preserve that historical metric with its exact label and add standard recall with the holdout-size denominator. Add deterministic surface replay for first impressions, actual visible and queued order, filters, refill, zero-history, imported history and cross-script matching.
